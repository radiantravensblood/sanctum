# 0002 — Identity Is a Proposal

## Question

Can an external system propose a name, self-description, role, preference, or goal without that proposal automatically becoming actor-owned truth or leaking into language-generation context?

## Upstream assumption being challenged

At pinned AI Town revision `5c91ce6f83cc62e1bc5bf2e3f51732c455064b9d`:

1. `data/characters.ts` stores developer-authored `name`, `identity`, and `plan` values;
2. `createAgent` copies `identity` and `plan` directly into `AgentDescription`;
3. `convex/agent/conversation.ts` injects those strings into the system prompt as `About you:` and `Your goals for the conversation:`;
4. no provenance, actor stance, revision history, or contest state exists between operator-authored text and prompt-visible self-state.

0002 does not claim that an artificial actor has subjective selfhood. The research question is architectural: can the system preserve the difference between metadata supplied by an operator and claims that an actor has explicitly authorized?

## Operational handle is not identity

The engine may require a stable routing identifier such as `p:0`. Familiar treats that as an operational handle, not as a personal name or self-description.

If no actor-authorized name claim is active, the prompt receives `actor:p:0` rather than silently adopting a developer-authored name.

## Claim model

Identity state is represented as versioned claims. A claim includes:

- `claimId`
- `actorId`
- `kind`: `name | self_description | role | preference | goal`
- `slot`
- `value`
- `proposedBy`
- `provenance`: `external_proposal | actor_revision`
- `status`: `proposed | deferred | active | declined | contested | superseded`
- event history and revision lineage

External proposals begin as `proposed` and are excluded from prompt projection.

## Actor decisions

An actor may:

- `accept` — activate the claim;
- `decline` — reject it without deleting the proposal;
- `defer` — leave it unresolved and non-projecting;
- `revise` — supersede the external wording with a new actor-authored active claim;
- `contest` an already-active claim later — preserve history while removing it from current prompt projection.

## Prompt law

Conversation generation may consume only the active authorized-identity projection. The legacy `AgentDescription.identity`, `AgentDescription.plan`, and `PlayerDescription.name/description` values may remain stored as upstream provenance, but the patched conversation prompt path must not read them as self-state.

The prompt receives an explicit authorized-state envelope generated by `identityPromptLines()`.

## Live witnesses

Reuse the deterministic non-agent witnesses from 0001:

- `p:0` — Familiar Witness A
- `p:1` — Familiar Witness B

The identity ledger and prompt projection can be tested without waking an autonomous AI Town Agent, keeping LLM behavior out of the causal chain.

## Falsification conditions

0002 fails if any of the following occur:

- a `proposed`, `deferred`, `declined`, `contested`, or `superseded` claim appears in prompt projection;
- an external proposer can authorize an actor-owned claim merely by being the proposer;
- an actor revision preserves the operator wording as the active claim instead of creating actor-authored lineage;
- contesting an active claim deletes the historical record rather than changing its stance;
- prompt projection falls back to legacy `identity`, `plan`, name, or description strings;
- the patched conversation prompt source still reads `agent.identity`, `agent.plan`, `agentDescription.identity`, or `agentDescription.plan`;
- no provenance path can distinguish external proposal from actor revision.

## Scope boundary

0002 governs identity claims and their conversation-prompt projection. It does not yet solve memory-derived self-beliefs, relationship-derived identity, autonomous goal formation, embodiment, or whether an actor should accept/revise claims through an LLM. Those remain later experiments.

## Success criterion

> Operator-authored text can exist as evidence without becoming self-state. Only active actor-authorized claims cross the prompt boundary.
